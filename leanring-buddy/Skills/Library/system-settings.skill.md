## Layout
- A sidebar on the left with a Search field at the top, then your name (Apple Account, called Apple ID before macOS 15), then the panes: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Network, Battery (laptops), General, Accessibility, Appearance, Control Center, Desktop & Dock, Displays, Wallpaper, Notifications, Sound, Focus, Screen Time, Lock Screen, Privacy & Security, Touch ID & Password, Users & Groups, Internet Accounts, Keyboard, Mouse, Trackpad, Printers & Scanners.
- The order of panes changes between macOS versions. When unsure, type the setting's name in Search.
- The right side shows the chosen pane. Many panes keep extra options behind buttons at the bottom, so scroll down.

## Where things are
- Update macOS: General > Software Update.
- Free up space: General > Storage.
- Apps that open at login: General > Login Items & Extensions (Login Items before macOS 15).
- Default browser: Desktop & Dock > Default web browser.
- Dark mode and accent colour: Appearance.
- Night Shift and resolution: Displays.
- Hot Corners: Desktop & Dock, button at the very bottom.
- App permissions: Privacy & Security, then Accessibility, Microphone, Camera, Full Disk Access, or Screen & System Audio Recording (Screen Recording before macOS 15).
- Keyboard shortcuts: Keyboard > Keyboard Shortcuts. Dictation: Keyboard > Dictation.
- Tap to click and scroll direction: Trackpad.
- AirDrop & Handoff, Sharing, Date & Time, Language & Region, Startup Disk, Transfer or Reset: all under General.
- Add a printer: Printers & Scanners > Add Printer, Scanner, or Fax.

## Gotchas
- After turning on a Privacy & Security permission for an app, that app usually has to be quit and reopened before it takes effect.
- Some switches ask for Touch ID or the Mac password first.
- Saved passwords moved to the separate Passwords app in macOS 15.
- A greyed-out setting on a work Mac is usually locked by the organisation's management profile.

## Shortcuts
- Cmd+F puts the cursor in Search.
- From any app: Apple menu > System Settings.
- Hold Option and press a brightness or volume key to open Displays or Sound directly.
